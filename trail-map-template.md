# [Name the thing]

A blank trail map. Copy this file, fill in the five headings, and keep it
in the folder next to the work. One page — around three hundred words. If
it will not fit, you are describing more than one job.

Delete the italic prompts as you go.

---

## 1. What it is

*One sentence, and who it is for. "It is a ___ for ___ that ___."*

*If you cannot finish that sentence, stop here. That is the actual
problem, and no amount of building will solve it.*

## 2. What it reads, and what it must never touch

*Where does the information come from today — a folder, a spreadsheet, a
notebook, somebody's head? Name it.*

*Then the nevers, plainly. It never sends anything. It never touches the
customer list. It never prices work we do not do. There are always at
least two.*

## 3. What you would actually see

*Describe the thing itself, not a category of thing. Do not write "a
summary". Write out one real line of it, the way it would look on the
screen or on the page.*

*This heading settles a dozen small arguments that would otherwise be
settled by whoever builds it, at eleven at night, without asking you.*

## 4. How you will know it is right

*Two or three things that must be true. Concrete ones, that somebody
could check by looking.*

*"The gas job is at the top." "Nothing I have already sent appears on
it." "It fits on a phone screen without scrolling."*

## 5. What I am not sure about

*The questions you cannot answer yet. Write them down even if — especially
if — you have no idea what the answer is.*

*This is the heading people leave out, and it is the most valuable one on
the page. A question written down is something you can go and find out on
Thursday. A question you never noticed is why the thing gets rebuilt in
March.*

---

When you have filled it in, open the folder with your assistant and say:

> Read this map. Don't build anything yet — tell me what's missing from it.

Almost nobody's first map survives that intact. That is the point of it.
